# Halden Dam — Season 1 Bible

A 10-episode anthology of original horror fiction. Each episode is a standalone story, told by a
different worker at the same place. The season has one mystery running through it, and the finale
answers it.

Format: 40–50 minute narration a week, three Shorts per episode (one rule each), and the complete
season as one video in week 11.

---

## The place

**Halden Dam** is a concrete gravity dam on the Halden River in the mountains of western Montana. It
was built between 1953 and 1957, and no public map shows it. The access road leaves Route 12 at
an unsigned gravel turnoff at **mile marker 57**. Staff are recruited through job listings that give
directions instead of an address.

**Halden Lake** is the reservoir. Under it lies the town of **Halden**, flooded when the reservoir
filled in the spring of 1957. Its church steeple sits at an elevation of **4,112 feet**.

**The official story:** 412 residents were relocated before the flooding.
**The 1950 census:** Halden had **413** residents.

## The truth (for writers only; the audience gets it in Episode 10)

The lake **keeps** things. Anything that goes under the water is held there exactly as it was, and
the lake can send up a copy. Halden was not evacuated in time. The whole town is still down there,
living its last day of April 1957 over and over. The copies want to come up and take the place of
whoever is standing on the dam.

A copy can only take your place if it has your **name**, your **place in the count**, or your
**consent** to come into the water. Every rule protects one of those three.

The dam is the seal. As long as the steeple stays underwater, the town stays down.

**Walter Ames**, the night supervisor, is the twist. He was the chief engineer in 1957. He
didn't age because he isn't the original Walter Ames; he's the first copy that ever came up. He
wrote the rules. They protect the staff, but they also keep the other copies down, so that he stays
the only one up here. Episode 10 is told by him.

## The 7 rules (master list)

Each worker gets the version of the rules that fits their job. Nobody gets all seven.

1. **Count the staff at every shift change. There are always 12.** If you count 13, do not count
   again. Leave the room and don't come back until the next shift.
2. **Never say your name where you can see the water.** On the radio, use your badge number.
3. **Walk the crest clockwise.** Never counterclockwise.
4. **If the lake is still at night with no wind, close every shutter that faces the water.**
5. **If you hear church bells, step away from the railing. Walk. Never run.**
6. **Nobody goes into the water after dark. If someone you know asks you to, it isn't them.**
7. **[REDACTED in every copy of the card since 1957.]**
   *The full rule:* "If the lake ever drops below 4,112 feet, everyone leaves. Nobody comes back up
   the road. Not even me." — W.A.

## Running motifs (one clue planted per episode)

| Motif | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **12 / 13** | The staff count, the 13th census resident, the copy among you |
| **57** | Mile marker 57, phone extension 57, the year 1957 |
| **4,112** | The height of the steeple, and the level the lake can never drop below |
| **Church bells** | A copy is close to the surface |
| **The narrator never says their name** | Every narrator, in every episode. Viewers will notice by Episode 3 |
| **Walter Ames looks the same in every decade** | Shown in photos, nobody mentions it until Episode 9 |

## Episode plan

| # | Title | Narrator | Rules this job gets | The clue it plants |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | I Guard a Dam That Isn't on Any Map. There Are 6 Rules. | Night security guard | 1–6 | A 1957 photo where Ames looks the same age; rule 7 is blacked out |
| 2 | I'm the Night Diver at Halden Dam. My Line Reached 900 Feet. | Intake diver | 2, 6, plus a diving rule: never follow a light | Streetlights on the lake bed are lit |
| 3 | I Work the Fire Tower Above Halden Lake. There Are 5 Rules. | Fire lookout | 3, 4, 5 | From above, the lake's still patches trace the town's streets |
| 4 | I Run the Radio at Halden Dam. 3 Calls Come From the Lake. | Radio operator | 2, plus extension 57 | One of the calls uses the narrator's badge number |
| 5 | I Cook at Halden Dam. We Have 12 Staff. I Feed 13. | Night cook | 1 | The 13th tray always comes back clean |
| 6 | I Inspect Tunnel 4 Under Halden Dam. It Reopens Every Night. | Tunnel inspector | 3, 5 | Tunnel 4 slopes down toward the town |
| 7 | I Tend a Cemetery Moved for a Dam. 4 Graves Came Back. | Groundskeeper at the relocated cemetery | 6 | The 4 returned headstones carry 1957 death dates and staff surnames |
| 8 | I Test Water at Halden Dam. 2 Samples Have Heartbeats. | Lab technician | 4, 6 | The lake level is dropping a few inches a week |
| 9 | I File Records at Halden Dam. Rule 7 Was Redacted in 1957. | Records clerk | All 6, and finds the original card | The 1950 census shows 413 residents |
| 10 | I Wrote the 7 Rules at Halden Dam. This Is Why. | Walter Ames | All 7 | The reveal; the lake is at 4,113 feet and falling |

**The thread that builds across episodes:** episodes 1–7 make the place feel real. Episode 8 starts
the countdown, because the lake is dropping. Episodes 9–10 pay it off. A new viewer can start on
any episode; a subscriber gets the whole picture.

## Season 2 seed

Episode 10 ends with the lake at 4,113 feet and still falling, and a new job listing for another
site with directions instead of an address.
